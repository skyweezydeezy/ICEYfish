    Mirror match beta

    Your deck plays another deck, or itself. Both sides use the same autopilot: attacks, blocks, life totals, and the first player to 0 life (or 21 commander damage) loses.

    Two-faced cards

    A card like Esika // The Prismatic Bridge is cast as one face or the other, chosen as it is cast.     Policy lab beta

    Learn by trial and error: the engine plays the same games with each option and keeps the one that does better. What it learns is saved in this browser for this deck and used by the autopilot.

    Tune to the next bracket

    Finds small changes that move the deck up a bracket by speed: the median goldfish kill turn before turn 4 is B5, before 6 B4, before 8 B3, before 9 B2. It cuts the cards that sit in your hand longest without being played for cards that speed the kill (ramp, card draw, finishers and combo pieces from your colors, one card for one so the deck stays 100 cards and legal), and tries the lab's choices (lands to keep, mulligans, when to cast the commander...). Every change plays the same shuffles as the deck now; a change is kept only when it beats chance, and the search goes on until the target is reached or nothing more helps. The answer is checked on 2,000 fresh shuffles the search never saw. Nothing changes in your deck until you press Apply.

    Tune toward a goal

    Make the deck kill the goldfish earlier, or hit its land drops more often. The lab measures the deck, then tries one change at a time: the choices above (how many lands to keep, mulligans, when to cast the commander…) and deck changes that keep its size (a land more for the slowest spell, cheap ramp or card draw in your colors for the slowest spell, ramp instead of a land). Every change plays the same shuffles, the one that moves the goal most is kept when it beats chance, and the next change starts from there. The deck before and after then plays fresh shuffles the search never saw: that is the before → after shown. A goldfish has no opponent, so interaction, combo pieces and win conditions are never cut; "Send to the A/B test" checks the new version against other decks. Bracket speed is the median goldfish kill turn, by Wizards' "turns played" guide and the same thresholds as the built-in decks' bracket check: before turn 4 is B5, before 6 B4, before 8 B3, before 9 B2, otherwise B1.

    Plays your deck against the opponents above (as many matches as the A/B test's Games setting), groups the cards linked to winning and losing by what they do (ramp, card draw, removal…) and says which kind of card the deck wants more of. It offers 2 cards of that kind to add and the 2 cards most linked to losing to remove; "Send to the A/B test" proves the change.

    A/B test beta

    Make the deck better one or two cards at a time. 1. Diagnose: the deck plays other decks and each card gets a verdict: card (fully simulated and cast, so the number is about the card), played (drawn but rarely cast: mana, curve or the autopilot's timing) or engine (some of its text is not simulated: test it by hand). 2. Press Cut on a card and pick a replacement from the list under it (popular cards that do the same job, in your colors), or write the changes yourself (one card per line, "- Card" to cut, "+ Card" to add), and test version 2 against version 1: the same opponents and shuffles, then fresh shuffles, then head to head. Better only when both runs point the same way and together pass z 2. A commander swap works too: a commander may shine in a different shell.

    How your saved data works

    Where it lives. Your saved decks, lab choices, tags, playmat and sound settings are kept in this browser, on this device. There is no account and nothing is sent to a server, so a different browser or device starts empty.

    Can it be lost? Yes, in a few cases: clearing your browser's history or website data, private browsing, uninstalling the browser, or a new device. Each browser also caps how much one website may keep (about 5 MB). Each browser keeps its own copy, so decks saved in one browser do not show up in another.

    iPhone and iPad. Safari deletes a website's saved data when the site has not been opened for 7 days. An ICEYfish app on your Home Screen (Share, then Add to Home Screen) is not affected, but it keeps its own storage, separate from Safari: it starts empty. Before you switch, use Export all in Safari and Import… in the Home Screen app (or send yourself a Share link), then use the Home Screen app from then on.

    Android. Chrome has no 7-day rule; it only clears a site's data by itself when the phone runs very low on storage. Installing ICEYfish (Add to Home screen, or Install app) uses the same saved data as the browser, so your decks are there either way. Samsung Internet, Firefox and Chrome each keep their own copy.

    Back up. Export all saves one file with every deck and your settings. Keep it in Files, iCloud Drive or Google Drive. To restore it, or to move to another device, use Import… and pick the file.

    Saving. With Save every deck I load on (below), any list you paste or import is saved here as soon as it loads, named after its commander. Built-in decks are always there and are not copied.

    Where you were. The open tab, the loaded deck, each tab's settings and the game on Practice come back when you reopen or reload ICEYfish. Reset (below) forgets them and starts fresh; your saved decks stay. An update of ICEYfish also starts fresh.

    Share a deck. Share link on a deck makes a link with the whole list inside it. Whoever opens it, or pastes it into the decklist box, gets the deck in their saved decks.
